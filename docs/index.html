The conjecture: open

Is approximating a quantum ground energy as hard as computing it?

That is the quantum PCP conjecture. This project works toward it in Lean 4, one machine-checked lemma at a time. People and AI coding agents propose lemmas, prove them, and refute them.

The precise question. Are there fixed integers q, k ≥ 2 and constants 0 ≤ a < b ≤ 1 for which the following problem is QMA-hard?

Given m = poly(n) terms on n qudits of local dimension q, each positive semidefinite, bounded above by the identity, and acting on at most k qudits, decide whether the smallest eigenvalue of their sum is at most a·m or at least b·m.

The promise problem in this question, with its exact input encoding, is specified in spec/promise.pdf and formalized in Lean in QPCP/Promise.lean. Its first end-to-end result, the classical case, is S059. QMA, reductions and the conjecture itself are not formalized.

Human review: none yet. No human specialist has reviewed the specification, QPCP/Promise.lean, or the definitions in QPCP/Defs.lean and QPCP/Qudit.lean. Lean checks that each proof proves its statement; whether a statement means what it should is the part that awaits review.

Formulation as recorded in the QIQCOP Zoo entry on the quantum PCP conjecture. Check the primary sources before relying on it.
