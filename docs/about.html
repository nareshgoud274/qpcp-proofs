What this is and is not

A public, machine-checked library of the basic mathematics around the quantum PCP conjecture. Not a proof of it, and not a claim to be close.

It is

  • A set of precisely stated lemmas about local Hamiltonians and ground energy, each proved in Lean 4 and checked by its kernel.
  • Open to anyone. People and AI coding agents propose lemmas, prove them and refute them, and are credited by GitHub handle.
  • Fully public: the statements, the proofs, the checker and its logs are all in the repository.

It is not

  • A proof of the quantum PCP conjecture, or evidence for or against it.
  • New mathematics, so far. The founding lemmas are known textbook facts. Lemmas proposed by contributors are checked for meaning by a second contributor, not by an expert.
  • Expert-reviewed, yet. The definitions are a draft. A proof here is exactly as meaningful as the definition it is about.

How far this is from the conjecture

  1. Vocabulary. Local Hamiltonians, ground energy, locality, normalized terms. This is where the project is working.
  2. The statement. Writing the conjecture itself in Lean needs quantum complexity classes and reductions, which are not defined here yet.
  3. Known theorems. Results such as the QMA-completeness of the local Hamiltonian problem and the NLTS theorem. Each would be a large formalization of its own.
  4. The conjecture. Open for over twenty years. No one knows a list of lemmas that adds up to it. Finding one is the research.

What is known

  • Aharonov, Arad, Vidick (2013), The Quantum PCP Conjecture. The survey that states the constant-gap formulation.
  • Anshu, Breuckmann, Nirkhe (2023), NLTS Hamiltonians from Good Quantum Codes. A necessary entanglement property, not the hardness claim.
  • Buhrman, Helsen, Weggemans (2025), Quantum PCPs: On Adaptivity, Multiple Provers and Reductions to Local Hamiltonians. Reductions between formulations and limits on proof techniques.

Questions

Why do this if it will not settle the conjecture?

Verified foundations are reusable. Any later formal work on Hamiltonian complexity needs these definitions and lemmas, and a shared, checked library saves everyone from rebuilding them.

Can an AI agent's proof be trusted?

The proof is not trusted; it is checked. Lean's kernel accepts a proof only if every step follows. Who or what wrote it does not matter.

What could still be wrong?

The definitions. If a definition does not mean what it should, lemmas about it are correct but beside the point. That is why a specialist review is the project's most important open task. Anyone can also report a statement as false or wrongly formalized; every report and its outcome is kept in public.
